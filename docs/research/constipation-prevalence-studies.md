# Published constipation prevalence studies: India and the US, data 1995-2025

Date: 2026-10-30. Research question: which published studies give constipation prevalence for India and the US? This file lists studies and numbers. It makes no series and no analysis.

## How this list was made
- **Search:** a background research agent (a model) found 48 studies and read them through the Europe PMC service. A second independent search with Muse failed: its quota ran out (API error 429), so there is no second searcher. Studies that another searcher would have found may be missing.
- **Checking:** Claude opened the papers behind 19 rows. The Verified column shows what Claude read: `full text`, `abstract`, or `agent only` (the agent's reading, not opened by Claude).
- **Corrections found by the check** (the agent's table was wrong or incomplete in these places; this file has the corrected text):
  - IN09 (Kerala): the agent said there is no split for functional constipation. Table 6 of the paper has splits by age, sex, education, and residence.
  - IN12 (12 cities): the paper's text says 19.7 percent and its Table 1 says 12.2 percent. The agent did not report this.
  - IN05 (Odisha): all 32 people with fewer than 3 stools per week were women.
  - IN01 (Haryana): the paper gives no definition of constipation for the 11.6 percent figure, and its start date differs between the abstract and the text.
  - M10 (Rome V): the paper gives no US constipation value, and India is not among its 15 countries.
- **Links:** for rows marked `abstract` or `agent only`, the link is a PubMed or DOI page. Claude read the same record through the Europe PMC service, not through that page.
- Percentages that the agent computed are marked "computed".

## What the studies show (facts only)
- **The number depends on the definition.** India: 2.6% (fewer than 3 stools per week, healthy people only, IN05), 11.6% (self-reported symptom, rural, IN01), 16.8% (Rome II) and 24.8% (self-report), Chandigarh, 2014 (IN02), 18.1% (Rome IV, Kerala, IN09), 0.5% (Rome III, Delhi school children, IN07, agent only). US: 3.1% (fewer than 3 per week) to 11.1% (Bristol type 1 or 2) in NHANES 2005-2010, 14.7% (EPOC 1997), 6.0% (Rome IV CIC, 2020), 40.1% (age 65+, Olmsted County, any constipation, 1996).
- **India has no national constipation series.** The national LASI survey reports a combined digestive outcome (18%) and splits for it by region, wealth, caste, and education. It does not report constipation alone (IN11).
- **Splits that exist for constipation itself:** sex (many studies, women higher), age (IN09: 9.3% below age 50 and 27.1% at 50 and over; US studies mixed), education (IN09; NHANES odds ratios), race (NHANES), residence (IN09). Income, caste, and region for constipation alone: none found for India.
- **Change over time in the same population:** US only. Olmsted County 1988 to 2003 (incidence, US02); a national online survey 2020 to 2022, 6.0% to 6.4% (US08). None for India.
- **Pooled estimates (pointers):** global 14% (M01, to 2010), Rome I 15.3% to Rome IV 10.1% (M02, 1990-2020), 12.14% for Rome II to IV (M06, 1999-2025), with India 2.47% from 4 studies (list of studies not opened) and North America 13.20%.

## Gaps (from the agent's lists, checked against the rows above)
**India**
- No national or state-level constipation study. 1995-2007: only IN13 (Pune, 1998) and IN03 (2008).
- Income, caste, region, and urban or rural for constipation alone: only IN09 (small, one district) has residence and education.
- Children: IN07 (0.5%, agent only) and hospital studies. Older adults: IN12 only. Kasthuri 2013 (rural Bangalore, elderly) was not opened, and no secondary number is used.
